Our Story · 1932 → Today

Same counter.
Same recipes.

Three Bowers — George, Richard, James — and ninety-four years of Lynn Road mornings.

George opened the shop in 1932. We have not really changed the way we do things since.

George Bowers opened the doors at 71 Lynn Road in 1932. The shop had a sawdust floor, whole carcasses hanging on hooks at the back, and a counter where Gaywood's housewives queued each morning before school drop-off. The sausages were mixed by hand in a bowl the size of a baby's bath.

By the late 1960s, George's son Richard had taken the apron and learned the recipe. He kept the same suppliers — local farms within a short drive of King's Lynn — and refined the sausage seasoning until people from outside the town were coming in on Saturday mornings to take half a dozen pounds home.

Today the shop is run by James, George's grandson and the third generation behind the counter. The recipes are the same. The cuts still come in whole and are broken down on the block. The hog roasts are still cooked overnight before a wedding, the way Richard's father did them, and the queue still forms on Saturday mornings.

We are not a supermarket. We are a butcher's shop. If you want a piece of meat cut a particular way, or a sausage made for a particular party, ask us. That is the whole point.
